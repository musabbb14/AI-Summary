INTRODUCTION  
These guidelines for the preparation of project report is designed to aid undergraduate students in the 
preparation of acceptable manuscripts and to serve as a procedural handbook for required steps leading to final 
approval of project report by Mathematics Department, Gombe State University, Gombe.  
These guidelines contain general requirements pertaining to style, organisation, format and reproduction which 
apply to all projects in Mathematics Department. 
 It is the students’ responsibility to study this document and use the content properly. In instances where 
instructions in whatsoever circumstances differ, this guide takes precedence.

PROGRAMMES IN MATHEMATICS DEPARTMENT  
All project reports must conform to the following guidelines: 
A. GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS 
1. All projects must be written in English. 
2.  It should be typed in 1.5 line spacing except for abstract, tables, indented quotations and 
footnotes which should be typed in single spacing leaving 1.5 inch margin on left side and 0.5 
inch on the right, top and bottom sides. 
3. The beginning of paragraphs should be indented. 
4. Try not to break words at the end of lines, justify (full justification) your typing throughout. 
Use font size 12, Times New Roman. 
5. A maximum of three-tier system should be adopted for the headings in the text.  
6. Table headings must appear above the table while the title of a figure must below the figure. 
7. Data presentation: The data used in drawing graphs and charts must be presented in work as 
appendices together with ethical clearance of the data.  
8. After completion of the project, candidates will have to undergo a preliminary oral defence to 
be arranged internally by the head of department at a convenient time before the end of 
Second Semester. 
9.  After the internal oral defence, the candidates will incorporate all the suggestions and 
corrections and then submit the revised and corrected copies of the same in loose bound to the 
department within one (1) week after the internal oral defence.  Final assessment will be done 
by the external examiner and thereafter the internal examiner. The last date for the submission 
of the revised and corrected project report will be informed by the Head of Department. 
10. While submitting the project the student MUST attach a covering letter from their supervisor 
certifying that the project confirms with all conditions stipulated in the approved guidelines. 
11. After completion of the final oral defence and assessment, the examiners will finalize the 
awards and submit the same to the Head of Department concerned. 
12. A candidate who fails the examination shall be treated according to the university rules.

6. Acknowledgement 
This should contain a brief note of appreciation to all those who contributed to the 
success of the study. 
7. Dedication 
This is to dedicate the work to any interest person who has indirectly contributed to 
the work or love one. 
8. Abstract 
The abstract should not exceed 200 words. It must be typed in single spacing and not 
indented. It should briefly indicate brief background of the work, the statement of the 
problem, the objectives, methodology, data collection and analysis, major findings/results and 
conclusion. 
9. Contents 
This is a list of section and subsections of the project report and indicating the pages 
as they occur in the body of the project. (This should be generated automatically) 
10. List of Tables/Figures/Plates/Appendices 
In the list of tables, figures, plates and appendices where the title runs more than one 
line, the subsequent line must be single spaced and not indented. 
NOTE:   
(a) All tables must be typed in single spacing and the titles of the tables must appear at the top 
extreme left of the table. All tables must be reflected in the body of the project. For example 
Table 1.1, Table 2.1, etc and must equally appear on List of Tables. 
(b) All figures must be properly/clearly drawn and the titles of the figures must appear at the 
bottom of the figure. All figures must be reflected in the body of the project. For example 
Figure 1.1, Figure 2.1, etc and must equally appear on the List of Figures. 
11. Abbreviations, Definitions, Glossaries and Symbols 
Explain all abbreviations and symbols. Define terms.

Introduction: This chapter should consist, at least, of the following sections: 
a. Introduction 
b. Statement of the problem 
c. Aim and Objectives 
d. Significance/Justification of the study 
e. Scope of the study 
f. Statement of hypothesis (if any), etc. 
Literature Review: This chapter reviews the study of others on the subject matter. It should be 
focused, logically arranged and up to date (i.e it should contain current articles) and the gap you wish 
to fill. 
Materials and Methods/Methodology: This chapter should describe the methodology of the work 
done and should take into consideration the study design (Conceptual/Structural Design), sampling 
methods, techniques used in data collection and in analysis of the data (i.e the method how each of the 
model can be achieved), model formulation. 
Results: This chapter presents the result and interpretation of analysis of data collected, observations 
made on information gathered in the materials and methods chapter. Explanation for the results 
obtained in the study. Published work on the subject should be consulted and acknowledged. 
Summary, Conclusions and recommendations: The summary will present highlights of the major 
findings. The conclusion gives the inferences drawn from the findings. The recommendations shall 
list possible ways of solving the problems identified by the work and areas for further exploration.

REFERENCES 
The reference style is the American Psychological Association (APA) format as describe 
below:   
(A) Text Reference 
1. All publications cited in the text should be presented in a list of references at the end of the 
project report in alphabetical order. 
2. A reference in the text should give only the author’s surname initials followed by the date of 
publication in brackets e.g  
 
One Work by One Author: 
- 
(2010) compared software methodology  
- 
In a study of software methodology comparison (Abubakar, 2010). 
 
One Work By Multiple Authors:

-

-

-

If the reference cited has more than two authors, the surname of the first followed by “et al” 
and then the date of the publication in brackets should be used. If the author’s name does not 
form part of a sentence in the text, the reference shall be enclosed in a bracket after the 
relevant word or at the end of the sentence of (Abubakar, 2000) or (Abubakar and Kabir, 
2014) or (Umoh et al., 2014). References should not be cited on footnotes. 
(B) Book References: References to books, brochures and book chapters  are given as follows: 
(i) 
If there is single author 
Mukhopadhyay, P. (1998). Theory and methods of survey sampling. Prentice-Hall of 
India Private Limited, New Delhi, India, pp 91-93. 
(ii) 
If two authors 
Hogg, R. V. and Craig, A. T. (1972). Introduction to Mathematical Statistics, 3rd 
Edition. Amerind Publishing Co. Pvt. Ltd., New Delhi, India, pp 226-228. 
(iii) 
Encyclopaedia or Dictionary 
Adamu, R. T. (2013). The Encyclopaedia of Computer Science (4th ed). New York. 
(C) Journal References: These should be written in the following forms:- 
(i) 
Single author 
Brass, W. (1958): The distribution of births in Human Populations. International 
Journal of Population studies. 12:51-72. 
(ii) 
Two authors 
Sheps, M.C., and Perrin, E. B. (1964): The distribution of birth intervals under a 
class of stochastic fertility models. Population studies. 17:321-331. 
Similar for multiple authors. 
(iii) 
Journal Article in Press 
Coke, U.O. and Arnold, T. (in press): Comparison Between Black Box and White 
Box Testing. Journal of Computer Science. 
 
(iv) 
Abstract as Original Source 
Bitrus, A., Sani, G.N., and Musa, Y,K, (2012). A Modelling Approach to Orphanage 
Information System. [Abstract]. Journal of Computer Science. 12:34 
(D) For proceedings: Those proceedings which are edited and published in the form of a book 
should be listed the same way as for a book. 
(E) For articles in a newspaper: Name of the writer should be indicated – if it is known 
otherwise it should be listed as Anonymous; e.g Bello, J. (2002). Devaluating the Kenyan 
Shilling. New Nigerian, February 3, P. 7. 
(F) Authors with the same surname 
K.A. Gomez (2013) and A.A. Gomez (2013) 
(G) Work with no Author: 
(Anonymous, 2014) 
(H) Groups as Authors 
The names of groups that serve as authors (e.g corporations, associations, government 
agencies and study groups) are usually spelled out each time they appear in a text citation. 
The names of some group authors are spelled out in the first citation and abbreviated 
thereafter.

D. SUBMISSION OF PROJECT REPORT 
(i) 
Prior to project examination 
Loose bound copy of the project report signed by the supervisor should be given to the 
Head of Department by the supervisor with a covering letter indicating that the project 
report is ready for external examination. The Head of Department will manage to fix a 
date for the oral examination and arrange to call the external examiner on that date to 
conduct the examination. 
(ii) 
Binding - After the project examination 
 The project should be permanently bound only after the oral examination making all 
necessary corrections and alterations. 
 The spine of each project should be lettered boldly in golden letters to indicate the degree, 
month and year and the name of the candidate. 
 Project binding should be in navy/dark blue colour.  
 The title of the project, name of the candidate should appear boldly on the front page, the 
registration number in parenthesis and date of submission should appear boldly in capital 
letters. 
E. NUMBER OF COPIES 
Four (4) or (5) copies of the approved project should be submitted to the Head of Department. 
Signed copies of the project are to be distributed as follows: 
1. University library 
2. Departmental library 
3. Supervisor I 
4. Supervisor II 
5. Candidate 
GRADING OF PROJECT 
Grading of the project is as follows; Supervisor 30%, Oral Defence 30% and External Examination 
40% and criteria for evaluation are attached for both the internal (oral defence) and external examiner 
evaluation. Every aspect is clearly indicated and mark (s) indicated at each stage.
